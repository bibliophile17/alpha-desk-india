Methodology

Event identification and timestamps

Each quarterly-results announcement is assigned to day 0, the first session in which the market can react. Announcements before 09:15 IST count on the same session; announcements after 15:30 IST count on the next session; weekend and holiday announcements roll to the next session. Announcements released between 09:15 and 15:30, and announcements with no recorded time on a trading day, are excluded from the primary study because daily closing prices cannot isolate an intraday reaction. Sessions are taken from observed exchange data, never inferred from calendar days. Duplicate company-quarter rows keep the earliest timestamp and the rest are logged.

Market model

Expected returns follow Ri,t = αi + βi Rm,t + εi,t, estimated by ordinary least squares on sessions [-130, -11] relative to day 0 (120 sessions, at least 100 usable). The estimation window ends ten sessions before the event so the event's own reaction cannot leak into α and β. Reaction days [-1, +5] of the same company's other announcements are removed from its estimation window. Abnormal return is ARi,t = Ri,t − (α̂i + β̂i Rm,t); cumulative abnormal return is CARi[a,b] = Σt=a..b ARi,t. A single AR is one day's surprise relative to the model; a CAR accumulates those daily surprises over the window. Returns are simple daily returns of dividend-adjusted closes (stocks) and of the price-index close (benchmark). Missing prices give missing returns; nothing is filled or set to zero.

Event windows and tests

CARs are computed over [0,+1], [0,+3], [0,+5] and, as a robustness window, [-1,+1]. The windows are nested and overlapping, so their results are not independent. Across events we report a one-sample t-test (sensitive to outliers and non-normality), a Wilcoxon signed-rank test (assumes a symmetric distribution), an exact sign test (ignores magnitude) and 95% confidence intervals. Companies reporting on the same day share market and sector shocks, so the primary p-value is date-clustered (cluster = day 0, G−1 degrees of freedom); it is unreliable when the number of date clusters is small. A common-shift placebo moves every event date by the same offset (±11 to ±50 sessions, 80 shifts), re-runs the pipeline, and compares the real mean CAR with the 80 placebo means (two-sided empirical p); it preserves same-day clustering but not within-company time-series dependence. Results need at least 30 events overall and 15 per sector to be labelled inferential; otherwise they are descriptive. Sector p-values are Holm-adjusted across inferential sectors only. Statistical significance is not economic significance and does not imply a profitable trading strategy.

Limitations

  • Survivorship bias. The universe is the current Nifty 100 constituent list applied to history; it does not represent past index membership.
  • Data source. Prices come from yfinance, an unofficial wrapper; adjustments for splits, bonuses and dividends were diagnosed but not independently reconciled with exchange data, and demergers and similar actions may not be modelled.
  • Benchmark gaps. Where the benchmark series lacks a session, that day is dropped from estimation and any event window touching it is voided rather than filled.
  • Announcement timing. Day 0 depends on the recorded announcement time; closing-auction effects for releases near 15:30 are not modelled.
  • Confounding. Market-wide, sector-wide and company-specific news in the same window is not separated from results.
  • Daily data. Closing prices cannot isolate intraday reactions.
  • Multiple testing. Four overlapping windows and several sectors are examined; unadjusted p-values overstate evidence.
  • Earnings measure. No analyst consensus is used; this study measures reaction to the announcement, not to an earnings surprise.

Future work: historical index membership, an official-data reconciliation of prices, consensus-based surprises, intraday data, alternative benchmarks and estimation windows.
